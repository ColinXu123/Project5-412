A bowl of ramen is a small, steaming universe.

Broth that simmers for hours, noodles with the perfect bite, and toppings that tie it all together. Here's everything we love about Japan's most famous comfort food.

Explore the styles

The four parts of every bowl

Foundation

Broth

Usually made from pork bones, chicken, fish, or vegetables, simmered anywhere from a few hours to a full day.

Flavor

Tare

A concentrated seasoning sauce at the bottom of the bowl. It's what makes a ramen shoyu, shio, or miso.

Texture

Noodles

Wheat noodles made with kansui, an alkaline water that gives them their springy chew and yellow color.

Finish

Toppings

Chashu pork, a jammy soft-boiled egg, scallions, nori, bamboo shoots, and a drizzle of aroma oil.

Fun facts

  • Ramen arrived in Japan from China in the late 1800s and was first called shina soba.
  • Instant ramen was invented by Momofuku Ando in 1958.
  • Slurping is encouraged: it cools the noodles and carries more aroma.
